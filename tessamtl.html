    🚇 Getting around

    Four colour-coded metro lines, running 5:30am–1am. Your station is Mont-Royal (orange line) — it's the hub for both the mountain and Old Montreal.

    Easiest: tap your contactless credit card or Apple/Google Pay right at the turnstile. $3.75 a ride, free transfer to a bus within 120 minutes. No card to buy.
    Lots of rides in one day? A day pass ($12) or 3-day pass ($22) may be cheaper — grab either at any station machine.

    ✈️ From the airport

    The airport train isn't running yet, so it's bus, taxi, or rideshare.

    Cheapest — Bus 747 ($11.25): runs 24/7. Buy your ticket right at the airport kiosk in the arrivals area — quick and easy. It includes a 24-hour transit pass, and drops you at Berri-UQAM station, a direct no-transfer ride up the orange line to the apartment. Budget 45–70 min.
    Easiest with luggage — Taxi: flat $41 downtown. No meter, no negotiating.
    Uber/Lyft: roughly $35–55 depending on demand.

    🍷 Buying alcohol here

    Two systems: the SAQ (government liquor stores) and grocery stores / dépanneurs (corner stores).

    SAQ — the only place for spirits (vodka, gin, rum, tequila, whisky) and the full range of wine and champagne. Staff know their stuff — just ask for something good in your budget.
    Grocery stores & dépanneurs — beer, cider, a limited wine selection, and low-alcohol coolers. No hard liquor at all, so don't go hunting for vodka at the corner store.
    Pro tip: for real selection look for an "SAQ Sélection" branch, not a tiny "SAQ Express". There's one 10 minutes from you at 1690 Ave du Mont-Royal E. Dépanneurs stay open later for a quick beer run. And some spots are BYOW ("apportez votre vin") — bring a bottle, save a lot.

    🎢 Adrenaline in the Old Port

    All three are on the same stretch of waterfront — you could do them back to back.

    Saute-Moutons — jet boat straight into the Lachine Rapids. You WILL get soaked (swimsuit + towel, seriously). 1 hour, from $65, runs through late September.
    MTL Zipline — Canada's only urban zipline, 1,200 feet over the Bonsecours basin at Hangar 16. Short and sharp.
    La Grande Roue — the tallest observation wheel in Canada. 18-minute rotation, 360° views, best at sunset. Student rate with ID is $24 (adult $29.50). Open 10am–11pm daily.

    🏔️ Mount Royal

    The mountain in the middle of the city. Worth doing properly once.

    Getting to the top: closest entrance is Park Avenue at Rachel, by the Sir George-Étienne Cartier monument — a 15–20 min walk from you. From there take Chemin Olmsted, a gently winding, well-marked path that climbs steadily; about 25–30 minutes uphill and the easiest route for a first visit. Skipping the climb? Bus 11 from Mont-Royal metro drops you 10 minutes from the lookout.
    Why bother: the Kondiaronk Belvedere in front of the chalet is the classic Montreal skyline view — the whole downtown below you, river beyond. There's a Leonard Cohen mural visible from up there, and the chalet has washrooms and a snack counter.
    Heads up: the Tam-Tams (Sunday drum circle) are cancelled Sept 6, your only Sunday, for the UCI cycling championships.

    🏛️ Old Montreal & the Old Port

    Cobblestones, 300-year-old stone buildings, right on the river. Totally different feel from the Plateau.

    Getting there: Mont-Royal station to Champ-de-Mars, same orange line, no transfer, about 15 minutes.
    Where to walk: start on Rue Saint-Paul (pedestrian, boutiques, terraces) → Place Jacques-Cartier (main square, street performers) → Marché Bonsecours (gorgeous 1847 building, Quebec-made boutiques) → down to the waterfront boardwalk for the view.

    Labour Day weekend lands mid-trip (Mon Sept 7) — expect a packed, patio-heavy city.

    🧀 Cheese curds, done right

    If you're bringing some home:

    The freshness test: real fresh curds squeak against your teeth. No squeak, not fresh. That's the whole test.
    Where: La Vieille Europe, an old-school European deli on St-Laurent, is a solid bet. A fromagerie or specialty grocer generally beats a supermarket — ask when the curds came in.
    Timing: they lose the squeak within a day or two even refrigerated. Buy close to when you pack, not on day one.
